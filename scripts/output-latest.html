Executive Summary

The quantum computing cybersecurity threat is real, present, and systematically underestimated 10. The data demonstrates that classical public-key systems (RSA, ECC) are vulnerable to Shor's algorithm, which solves integer factorization and discrete logarithms in polynomial time on a sufficiently capable fault-tolerant quantum computer 4. This is not a speculative future risk; the 'harvest now, decrypt later' threat model means adversaries can record ciphertext today and decrypt it later, making the cryptographic threat present, not future 10. The literature indicates that well-funded actors are currently capturing and storing massive amounts of encrypted data, waiting for the day a quantum computer can unlock it 11. This analysis proposes that the central challenge is not whether quantum computers will break cryptography, but whether the global ecosystem can migrate to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) before a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) arrives 10. The evidence indicates that enterprise readiness is dangerously low: 94 percent of enterprise systems assessed are today still using quantum-vulnerable cryptographic primitives, and less than 12 percent of organizations surveyed have started formal planning efforts for quantum migration 10. This collective action problem is compounded by the fact that PQC migration requires larger parameter sizes, hybrid cryptographic schemes, and unprecedented ecosystem coordination compared to past transitions like AES, SHA-2, and TLS 1 23.3 23. We project that without immediate, coordinated action, the window for safe migration narrows, increasing the likelihood of a 'crypto winter' scenario where a significant fraction of encrypted data becomes exposed 4. The verdict is firm: the threat is imminent in its consequences, even if the exact timing of CRQC arrival is uncertain. The question is not whether to act, but how quickly and comprehensively. The analysis acknowledges that current quantum hardware is still a considerable distance from achieving the CRQC threshold 3, but the steady progress in technology indicates that the realization of CRQCs depends fundamentally on ongoing advances in scaling and engineering 3. Therefore, the prudent course is to accelerate migration now, prioritizing long-lived data and critical infrastructure, while monitoring quantum hardware progress to adjust timelines as evidence evolves 4.

The blockchain sector will face a paradox: its immutability makes it vulnerable to harvest-now-decrypt-later, but integrating PQC may increase latency and reduce throughput, challenging the scalability trilemma 81. Overall, the trajectory is one of increasing urgency and partial preparedness, with a real risk of a 'crypto winter' for sectors that delay migration 12.
